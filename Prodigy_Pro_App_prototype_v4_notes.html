1Investment horizon and the risk profile

RIA view: if horizon is baked into the risk profile, a later goal with a different tenure gets a wrong suggestion. Profile on 7 factors instead, and ask horizon every time we suggest something.

Both sides are right, about different things. Horizon is a mandatory input for suitability, so your “golden standard” point holds. But it describes a pot of money, not the person. One investor can have a 2-year trip and a 24-year retirement at the same time, so no single horizon in their profile can be right for both.

How to justify it to compliance

  • SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, Reg. 16 (risk profiling) asks the adviser to obtain information including the client’s investment objectives, how long they wish to stay invested and the purpose of the investment. It requires us to collect and use horizon. It does not say horizon must be a weight inside one person-level score. (Please have compliance confirm the current wording.)
  • Asking for horizon at each point of advice, and storing it with that recommendation, gives a stronger audit trail: every suggestion records the profile and the horizon it was based on.
  • The RIAs’ seven factors line up closely with the other items Reg. 16 lists: age, objectives, income, existing investments, risk appetite and liabilities. Horizon and purpose are the two that belong to the goal.

Why not “recalculate the risk profile for each goal”?

We considered prompting “we need to recalculate your risk profile for this goal” and recommend against it:

  • Risk tolerance and capacity are properties of the person. If the profile changes every time a goal is created, the person ends up with several “profiles”, which confuses users and auditors.
  • It double-counts: the horizon would sit inside the score and in the goal tenure.
  • It adds friction (re-answering questions) at the moment the user is most motivated to invest.

Instead: the profile stays fixed and the mix is recalculated. Target mix = f(risk profile, horizon of this money).

2Equity / debt / gold recommendations and the 2×2 review

RIA view: holdings change daily, sometimes a lot in 1–6 months, and each fund has its own mix. So drop equity / debt / gold-wise recommendations.

Right diagnosis, but dropping allocation is the wrong fix. Asset allocation is what makes a recommendation suitable and what drives rebalancing, so we can’t advise without it. What has to go is the shortcut of treating a fund’s category as its asset class.

Why the drift concern is real but bounded

  • AMCs disclose full portfolios monthly (debt schemes more often). Holdings change daily, but the latest disclosure is the best available input and it is refreshed every month.
  • For most equity and debt categories, SEBI’s categorisation rules cap the drift. For example, large cap funds keep at least 80% in large caps and flexi cap funds at least 65% in equity.
  • The big movers are a small set we can treat specially: balanced advantage / dynamic asset allocation (0–100% equity), multi-asset, equity savings, fund of funds, and funds holding high cash.

What v4 does instead

  1. Look-through mix. The portfolio’s equity, debt & cash and gold come from what each fund actually held at the last disclosure, stamped “as of 30 Sep 2026”. In the sample portfolio, look-through equity is 87% while category labels would say 92%.
  2. Bands, not points. Each target is ±5%. We act only when the portfolio is outside the band, so monthly drift doesn’t trigger churn. Even then, the first step is to redirect new SIPs, not to sell.
  3. Model portfolios, not buckets. For each profile × horizon the research team publishes a fund list with weights. The app shows the funds and one check line: “Looking inside these funds, about 45% is in equity today, within the 40–50% that suits money you need in 3 to 5 years.” Funds are never labelled “your equity fund”.
  4. Dynamic funds carry a range (“moves between 30% and 80% equity”) and are re-checked monthly.

What the industry does

  • Category-based split (fund category → asset class) is common in transactional apps. It is simple but wrong for hybrids and FoFs.
  • Look-through / X-ray (Morningstar Portfolio X-Ray, Value Research’s portfolio tools) uses disclosed holdings. This is the standard for advice.
  • Data vendors such as Morningstar, Value Research, ACE MF, CMOTS and ICRA Analytics supply holdings-level data and look-through splits.

Please verify current competitor behaviour before quoting it externally; app features change often.

The 2×2 scheme review

The RIAs’ four labels work well as a 2×2, but a 2×2 needs two named axes. We used performance against the fund’s own category (across) and fit with this investor’s plan (up):

SwitchWeak performance, good fit. The job is right but the fund is wrong: move to a better fund in the same role.
SatisfactoryStrong performance, good fit. Keep it and keep the SIPs going.
RedeemWeak performance, poor fit. Exit, timed for the lowest tax.
Under watchStrong performance, poor fit (overlap, overweight, too risky for the goal). Don’t add money; trim when rebalancing.
← weakerPerformance vs categorystronger →
  • Performance = rank of 3-year rolling returns in its SEBI category over the last 12 quarters, plus downside capture. These are single-scheme metrics, so the RIAs agree they are valid.
  • Fit = overlap with other holdings, weight against its band, risk against the linked goal’s horizon, and purpose (for example, ELSS for someone on the new tax regime).
  • Anti-churn rule: a fund moves to Switch or Redeem only after two quarters in a row in the bottom half.
  • v4 has two views: a Summary grid with count, value and fund-house badges per quadrant, and an Each fund bubble chart (size = amount held, tap to open).

3Tax on switch and redemption

Agree, and it should appear wherever we suggest selling: redeem, switch, rebalancing actions, Smart Withdraw and the holding page. A switch is a sale for tax, even within the same fund house.

Equity-oriented (65%+ Indian equity, incl. arbitrage and balanced advantage)Up to 12 months: 20%Over 12 months: 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 L a year

Plus 4% cess and any surcharge. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 Apr 2026, so section references change; we have assumed rates carry over. Please have a tax adviser confirm before launch.

What the engine does

  • Lot-level FIFO per folio and scheme: oldest units are sold first, as the law requires. Each SIP instalment has its own holding period and, for ELSS, its own 3-year lock-in.
  • Set-off: short-term losses against any gain; long-term losses against long-term gains only. Then the ₹1.25 L exemption, net of gains already booked this financial year.
  • Exit load per lot, shown separately. Exit load is deducted by the AMC; tax is not deducted for resident investors and is paid at filing. The UI says “Reaches your bank” and “Estimated tax” separately for this reason.
  • Combined effect: on “Review changes”, the total tax considers all selected sales together, so booking the Tata Digital loss reduces tax on the small-cap trim.

Data needed for production

  • Transaction-level history (purchase date, units, NAV) per folio, from the CAS, RTA feeds (CAMS / KFintech) or Account Aggregator MF data. Holdings alone are not enough.
  • Gains already realised this financial year (from the capital gains statement), to know how much of the ₹1.25 L is left.
  • The user’s tax slab (ask once, editable; v4 assumes 30%), residential status (NRIs have TDS), and grandfathered cost for equity units bought before 31 Jan 2018.
  • Pledged units (loan against MF) can’t be redeemed and must be excluded.

Also worth adding: a gain-harvesting nudge in Feb–Mar (“you can book ₹13,000 more tax-free before 31 March”). v4 already shows this in the Tax snapshot.

4Redemption assistant → Smart Withdraw

Why this name: “withdraw” is the user’s word (“I need ₹1 lakh”); “redeem” is industry jargon. “Smart” tells them the app is choosing for them. Alternatives: Tax-smart Withdrawal, Withdraw Wisely, Cash-out Planner.

Logic

  1. Inputs: amount, purpose (planned expense / emergency / goal due / other), and a toggle to leave goal-linked funds alone.
  2. Constraints: FIFO within each fund, ELSS lock-in, pledged and in-process units are unavailable.
  3. Cost of each next ₹ from each fund = tax + exit load for the next FIFO units, adjusted by plan preferences:
    • Redeem and Switch quadrant funds go first, then Under watch.
    • Loss-making units are attractive because they offset gains.
    • Long-term equity gains are free until the ₹1.25 L limit is used.
    • Goal-linked funds are penalised when the toggle is on.
    • The emergency cushion (liquid / arbitrage) is penalised unless the purpose is an emergency.
    • When equity is above its band, equity funds get a small preference.
  4. Greedy allocation in small steps, each step taking the cheapest fund. The output is grouped by fund, with a reason for each.
  5. Proof of value: a side-by-side with “sell evenly from every fund” (tax, exit load, number of funds touched), plus a list of what was left untouched and why.
  6. Alternatives: for a short-term need, suggest a loan against MF; for an emergency, explain payout timing and point to instant redemption from liquid funds.

Sample result: for ₹1 L the plan sells the underperforming ELSS (long-term units only) and the loss-making Tata Digital fund. Estimated tax is ₹0, against ₹1,700 when selling evenly.

Production edge cases: amount above what’s redeemable, joint or minor folios, demat-held units, NRI TDS, cut-off times, and partial-unit rounding.

5Advisory headline

Agree. A scheme-level headline is more actionable than a rupee loss in a hypothetical fall. Two tweaks:
  • Say “underperforming their category”, not “non-performing”. “Non-performing” sounds like a bad loan (NPA) and is undefined.
  • Show the money share next to the count. Two small funds out of nine is a different story from two large ones.

v4 copy: “22% of your schemes are underperforming their category: 2 of 9, holding 14% of your money. 2 more are on watch. 5 changes could lift your score to 82.”

Risk fit is still shown lower down, as a band (“Equity is 15% above your band”), because suitability against the profile is part of our RIA duty. It is no longer the headline.

7Sharpe, Alpha, Beta, Sortino for the whole portfolio

They can be calculated, and portfolio X-ray tools routinely do this. What can’t be done is to average fund-level ratios from factsheets. Funds don’t move in step, so portfolio volatility is less than the weighted average of fund volatilities. That is probably the concern behind the RIAs’ point.

Method (to share with the RIAs)

  1. Build the portfolio’s return series. Either holdings-based (today’s weights × each fund’s monthly NAV returns, for 36+ months, which answers “how risky is what I hold now”), or returns-based (time-weighted return of the actual account, which strips out SIP and withdrawal cash flows).
  2. Use a matching benchmark: the investor’s target blend, for example 67% Nifty 500 TRI + 23% CRISIL Composite Bond + 10% gold. A pure equity index makes a mixed portfolio look artificially safe.
  3. Calculate on that series:
Volatility σp = stdev(monthly Rp) × √12 Sharpe = (Rp − Rf) / σp Sortino = (Rp − Rf) / downside deviation (months below Rf only) Beta βp = cov(Rp, Rb) / var(Rb) Alpha (Jensen) = Rp − [Rf + βp × (Rb − Rf)]
  • Beta is the exception: portfolio beta is the value-weighted average of fund betas, if every beta is measured against the same benchmark over the same period. Volatility, Sharpe and Sortino do not average.
  • Caveats: at least 36 monthly observations; funds younger than that need a shorter common window or a category proxy; XIRR is for the investor’s return, not an input to these ratios.

In v4: the table compares “Yours” with “Benchmark” (your blend) instead of the old “Suits you” column, which was not meaningful for alpha or Sharpe. Worst fall and downside capture are added because users understand them, and a “How it’s calculated” sheet explains the method.

Open questions for the RIAs and compliance

  1. Compliance: is capturing horizon per goal / per recommendation, and not in the profile score, acceptable under Reg. 16 and our risk-profiling policy? How should we store it in the advice record?
  2. Investment committee: sign off the profile × horizon equity table, the ±5% band and the gold rule.
  3. Risk questionnaire: exact answer options and scoring for the 7 factors. Should “dependants” come back as a capacity factor? Is “lower of tolerance and capacity” the rule we want?
  4. Scheme review: exact definitions and thresholds for “performance” and “fit”, and the anti-churn rule (2 quarters in the bottom half).
  5. Model portfolios: who curates the fund list per profile × horizon, and how often it changes.
  6. Data: which vendor supplies look-through holdings and category ranks, and do we get lot-level transactions via CAS / RTA / AA for every folio?
  7. Tax: tax adviser to confirm FY 2026-27 rates under the new Act; how we ask for the user’s slab; NRI handling.
  8. Smart Withdraw: is the order of preference right? Should goal-linked funds ever be sold automatically?
